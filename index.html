The employability gap

The experience paradox

Entry-level supply chain vacancies routinely require prior operational experience, yet very few roles offer the exposure needed to acquire it. This experience paradox is the barrier the programme is designed to dismantle.

For career changers the barrier is sharper still. Many bring proven transferable strengths (stakeholder management, problem resolution, commercial judgement and organisational discipline) but cannot yet evidence a working command of how a customer order is converted into a delivery, an invoice and, ultimately, cash. Without that evidence, recruiters discount capability that is genuinely there, and strong candidates are screened out before interview.

What actually separates hired candidates

Selection rarely turns on raw ability. It turns on whether a candidate can answer three questions with precision, using real transactions and real consequences as evidence:

  1. How does the end-to-end order-to-cash process operate, and where are value and risk created?
  2. Where does your role sit, and which upstream and downstream teams depend on your work?
  3. How would you diagnose and resolve an exception when the process breaks down?

The programme is engineered in reverse from those three questions: every module, every placement task and every mentoring responsibility exists so that you can answer them with authority.

Hands-on

Your workplace: VMErp

You will not learn the process from slides alone. Every module is practised in VMErp, the ERP system VME Pumps Ltd uses to run its business: its orders, production, purchasing, stock and invoices.

Participants work in VMErp Training, at training.vmerp.co.uk. It is the same application as the live system at vmerp.co.uk, with its own data and an orange “Training system” banner. What you practise is what the business actually does, and nothing you do there affects live customers or stock.

The complete cycle

Enquiry, quotation, sales order, production plan, purchasing, goods receipt, works order, delivery, invoice and payment, all in one system.

Document flow

Every transaction linked from quotation to payment, so you can open any document and see exactly where an order is.

Real-world situations

Rush and project orders, part shipments, stock transfers, proforma invoices, and returns with credit and debit memos.

Work instructions

Step-by-step instructions for every process, written for the role that carries it out.

Business documents

PDF quotations, order confirmations, delivery notes and invoices on VME letterhead, with weights, commodity codes and Incoterms.

Reports and analytics

Excel reports grouped by Sales, Purchasing, Manufacturing and Finance, and a dashboard you can customise.

Controls and checks

Warnings when a step is attempted out of sequence, and flags on documents that should not exist on their own.

Guided exercises

Practise, make mistakes and repeat them in the training system without affecting the live business.

Why a real ERP system, and why this one

Employers ask for ERP experience because they want people who already think in documents, statuses and handoffs: who know that a delivery cannot be invoiced before it exists, and that a cancelled order has consequences downstream.

VMErp uses the same building blocks as SAP, Microsoft Dynamics, Oracle and ERPNext: sales orders, purchase orders, goods receipts, works orders and document flow. Once you have run the whole cycle in VMErp, picking up your employer’s system means learning new screens, not a new way of working.

Excel for everyday work

Not confident with spreadsheets? Participants who need it receive practical Excel training for day-to-day tasks: building and filtering lists, formulas and lookups, pivot tables, and simple reports and charts. You then use those skills on real VMErp data, such as Excel exports of orders, stock and invoices.

Skills that travel beyond supply chain

The skills you build are transferable to any establishment, not just supply chain. Understanding how an organisation sells, buys, produces and gets paid; working confidently in an ERP system, Excel, email and shared drives; communicating with colleagues and customers; solving problems; and leading others are valued in operations, administration, customer service, finance and project roles across every sector.

The cross-training argument, briefly

Most operational errors happen at the handoffs: an order priced wrong that Finance has to credit, a delivery date promised that Manufacturing cannot meet, a purchase raised too late for production. Every one of those is a failure of one function not understanding another’s constraints. A workforce trained across all four functions makes fewer of them.

    Frequently asked questions

    Do I need supply chain experience?

    No. No prior experience is assumed and no supply chain background is required. Every module begins in plain business language before it becomes specific to any function.

    Will I receive a certificate?

    Yes. When you have completed and satisfied all three areas (Learn, Work and Mentor), you receive the VME Career UK Certificate: Supply Chain Specialist. It names each area you satisfied, so employers can see what it stands for.

    Will VME Career UK give me a reference?

    Yes. When you complete the programme, VME Career UK provides a reference to support your job applications, alongside your Supply Chain Specialist Certificate.

    Will the programme guarantee me a job?

    No. No training programme can guarantee a job, and this one does not claim to. What it does is make sure you understand the work, can evidence that understanding, can present it well, and are not on your own while you look.

    What is VME Pumps Ltd?

    VME Pumps Ltd is the business you learn in and then work for: a UK manufacturer and distributor of industrial pumps, with a manufacturing site in Manchester and a distribution centre in Birmingham. Training uses VME's own products, customers and suppliers throughout, so you see how a real supply chain fits together rather than meeting disconnected examples. See www.vmepumps.co.uk.

    How do the Learning Platform and VMErp work together?

    Each module on the VME Learning Platform explains a process, shows it in real VMErp screenshots and gives you the work instruction. You then open VMErp Training and do the same task yourself, creating real numbered documents. Quizzes, the document flow and Excel reports let you check your work. One username and password opens both.

    Is the work at VME paid?

    No. In Stages 2 and 3 you are employed by VME on an unpaid basis, for work experience. You do real day-to-day supply chain work under an experienced manager until you can do it with expert confidence, then continue that experience while mentoring new participants. The value is the experience, the evidence for your CV and interviews, and the support into a paid 9–5 hybrid or remote role.

    Do I need Excel skills?

    No. If you are not confident with spreadsheets, you receive practical Excel training for day-to-day tasks, including lists, formulas, lookups, pivot tables and simple reports, and then use it on real VMErp data.

    Are the skills only useful in supply chain?

    No. The skills are transferable to any organisation: business process understanding, ERP and Excel, professional communication, problem-solving and team leadership are valued in operations, administration, customer service, finance and project roles in every sector.

    Is this software training?

    No. It is business-process training. You learn how a business sells, builds, buys and gets paid. You practise in VMErp, VME’s own ERP system, which uses the same concepts as SAP, Dynamics and ERPNext. The aim is to understand the process, not to configure the software.

    What is VMErp?

    VMErp is the ERP system VME Pumps Ltd uses to run its orders, production, purchasing, stock and invoices. You work in VMErp Training, a copy of it with its own data, exercises and work instructions, so you can practise every process without affecting the live business. You receive your sign-in when you enrol.

    What will I be expected to do?

    Work through the modules in order, complete the practical activity in every module, take part in the job description workshops from the start, do mock interviews, and reach Stage 3 to mentor someone else. Mentoring is a requirement, not an optional extra.

    Is support available after I start work?

    Yes. Support continues while you apply, in your first weeks in a role, through your first year, and as you progress. You also keep access to programme materials and VMErp Training as they are updated, and to the participant network.

    What if the programme is not right for me?

    We will say so. Enrolment begins with a conversation precisely so that you and we can decide whether this is genuinely the right route.
